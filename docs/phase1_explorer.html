The idea in four lines

  1. Problem: SPAVM reuses containers. A hacked RSU corrupts every chain that reuses its container, and it lies that it is healthy.
  2. Evidence: the controller never trusts self-reports. It uses test packets (probes), end-to-end checks of chains, and catching lies.
  3. Score: evidence becomes a trust score T between 0 and 1 for every RSU (Beta reputation).
  4. Gate: SPVIR and VIMA skip any RSU whose trust is too low. Result: poisoned chains 29.4% → 1.2%.

Who gets blamed when a chain fails?

The end-to-end check only says "this chain failed". It cannot see which RSU did it. So 1 unit of blame is split over the RSUs on the chain, more to those already less trusted: weight = (1 − T) + 0.001. Move the sliders.

Success instead? Each RSU simply gets good + 1/k, equal shares.

RSUTweight (1−T)+0.001blame sharebad added (× ρ = 3 at end of slot)

Why this way: the RSU already under suspicion is the most likely culprit. An honest RSU that shares a chain with a hacked one still gets a little blame, which is the main cause of the ~4.4% false blocks.

Questions he may ask

Why not just ask the RSU if it is healthy?
A hacked RSU always says yes. We use only evidence the controller checks itself, and a caught lie becomes extra evidence against it.
Why does 20% hacked give 29% poisoned?
Reuse spreads the damage: one container serves up to 4 chains per slot, and a chain passes 2–6 VNFs on several RSUs, so many chains touch at least one hacked RSU.
Why Beta reputation?
It is the standard reputation model (Jøsang & Ismail 2002). T = (a+1)/(a+b+2) is the mean of Beta(a+1, b+1); it starts at 0.5 = unknown and needs only two counters per RSU.
What stops an on-off attacker?
ρ = 3: one bad slot cancels about three good ones, and γ keeps the memory for several slots, so the RSU cannot rebuild trust in its 5 "good" slots.
Why are honest RSUs sometimes blocked?
Random faults (2%) and shared blame on failed chains. About 4.4% of honest RSU-slots; they recover through passed probes, usually in one slot.
What did you change in SPAVM itself?
Only a trust check in three places: SPVIR reuse, SPVIR cold start, VIMA target. With trust off, results are identical to the baseline (tested).
What did you try and reject?
A trust-based sharing limit (Step 5) and quarantine. The sharing limit added 30–45% delay with no safety gain; quarantine gave the same result as the gate.
What are the limits?
The controller is assumed honest; a hacked RSU can't tell probes from real traffic; attackers must be a minority. Parameters are our choices, not from SPAVM.
